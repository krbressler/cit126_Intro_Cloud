Where Does the Cloud Put Its Stuff?

When you save a file on your laptop, it goes to your hard drive. But in the cloud, we have different types of storage depending on what we're saving, how often we need it, and how fast we need to get it back.

Let's review the "Big Six" storage options you need to know for the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner exam. Review my study cards below, and then test your knowledge in the Scenario Challenge!

Study Cards

Amazon S3

Simple Storage Service

Object Storage. Think of it as an infinite, flat warehouse. Every file (object) gets a unique URL. Great for backups, images, and static websites.

💡 Analogy: A massive valet parking lot where every car gets a ticket.

Amazon EBS

Elastic Block Store

Block Storage. High-performance storage attached to a single EC2 instance (virtual server). Perfect for databases and operating systems.

💡 Analogy: The C: Drive or internal hard drive plugged into your laptop.

Amazon EFS

Elastic File System

File Storage. Scalable storage that can be attached to multiple EC2 Linux instances simultaneously. Scales automatically as you add files.

💡 Analogy: A shared office network drive that everyone's computer connects to.

S3 Glacier

Cold Archive Storage

A storage class of S3 designed for long-term archiving and data compliance. Extremely cheap, but retrieving data can take minutes to hours.

💡 Analogy: A deep freeze in your basement or a bank vault. Hard to get to, but safe.

Storage Gateway

Hybrid Cloud Storage

Connects your on-premise, physical servers to AWS cloud storage. It bridges the gap between your local office and the AWS Cloud.

💡 Analogy: A magic portal connecting your office basement to the AWS cloud.

Snow Family

Physical Transfer

Physical devices (Snowcone, Snowball, Snowmobile) used to securely migrate petabytes of data into AWS without relying on slow internet speeds.

💡 Analogy: Hiring a fleet of armored trucks to move your filing cabinets.
